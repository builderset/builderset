_This page describes Builderset 0.4.0._

At the end a Django app stores email templates, articles, forms, card designs and
popups, with who made, changed and deleted each row and when; keeps their revisions
and a log of what happened to them; refuses an unsafe or stale save with the same
issue codes as the editor; takes form responses and popup submissions checked exactly
as the browser checked them; and issues cards to members. The tables and routes are
the ones in
[Data model and API](/docs/guides/backend/data-model); the checks come from the
`builderset` package, whose install is in [Python](/docs/reference/python):

<include cwd lang="sh" meta='title="Terminal"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/install.sh
</include>

The files below are two Django apps: `accounts`, your users, and `documents`, the
rest. They need Django 6.0 or later, whose foreign keys can let the database itself
set a removed user's columns to null. Add to your settings:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="settings.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/settings.py
</include>

And `documents/models/__init__.py` imports each model, so `from .models import Form`
works anywhere in the app.

Where the tables differ from the SQL: Django keeps `updated_at` itself, `auto_now` on
`save()` and `update_by()` on a queryset update, so there is no trigger, and a change
made in `psql` leaves it as it was. Its `Now()` is `statement_timestamp()`, not
`now()`; `sha256` is a `varchar(64)`; the revision index of email templates is
`email_template_revision_hist`, because Django caps index names at 30 characters;
the card number's unique index comes with a unique constraint of the same name, and
`card_issue_one_valid` reads `not status = 'revoked'`, the same rule; and its foreign
keys are checked at commit. Each builder also has a `publish_*` permission beside
Django's own add, change, delete and view: for a card design it is the approval for
print, for a popup it covers pausing too.

### Saving a draft

The editor sends back the `saved_at` it last saw. A different one on the row means
someone else saved in between: `409`, and the editor offers to reload or keep its
own. The `update()` is filtered on the `saved_at` read, so two saves at the same
instant cannot both win. An autosave is not history; a manual save keeps a revision
and logs `saved`:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="documents/views/documents.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/documents/views/documents.py#draft
</include>

</Step>
<Step>

### Publishing

The strict check, then the document goes live with its meta copied to the columns,
its `version` counted, a `publish` revision kept and the change of status logged.
An email type keeps one active design; an article with a future `scheduled_at` waits
for a job of yours to set it live; a form's address taken by another is refused on
`meta.slug`, where the editor shows it:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="documents/views/documents.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/documents/views/documents.py#publish
</include>

Unpublishing leaves a form `closed` — its link keeps working and shows the closed
message — and anything else a draft, a paused popup too. A live document cannot be
deleted —
`DELETE` answers `409` until it is unpublished — and a deleted one answers `404`
everywhere but `restore`.

</Step>
<Step>

### The form's public routes

The answers are checked against the **published** document, never the draft; a
filled trap field is answered as a success and stored nowhere; and only the `values`
that `validate_answers` keeps are stored, so an answer to a question the visitor was
not shown never reaches the table. A signed-in visitor is named in `created_by`. The
count and the insert share a transaction, so `maxResponses` holds when two visitors
send the last response at once:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="documents/views/forms.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/documents/views/forms.py#public
</include>

The throttle answers `429` past the rate in
`REST_FRAMEWORK["DEFAULT_THROTTLE_RATES"]["form_responses"]`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Cards

A card is issued from a published design only — `422` while it is a draft — and from
its live version: `fields_used` names the member's data it prints, every one is
required, and only those are frozen into the issue. The answer carries the card as it
is printed, the design filled in by `fill_card`. A member holds one card of a design
that is not revoked: the partial unique index `card_issue_one_valid` refuses a second,
answered `409`. Revoking keeps the row, so the number printed on the card still points
at it:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="documents/views/cards.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/documents/views/cards.py#issue
</include>

</Step>
<Step>

### The popups' public routes

Your site asks which popups may show on a page; the server answers from the indexed
columns, the dates, then the page and language rules with the kit's `path_matches` and
`language_matches`, highest priority first. `should_show` — `shouldShow` in the
browser — decides the rest: the audience, how often, the device. Events add to the
day's counters in one upsert; a submission is checked with `validate_submission`
against the live popup, and only its `values` are stored:

<include cwd lang="python" meta='title="documents/views/popups.py"'>
  ./snippets/backends/django/documents/views/popups.py#public
</include>

`PATCH /api/popups/:id/pause` takes `{ paused }` and answers `{ status }`; it changes
only the status, so the document and `saved_at` stay as they are, and a draft answers
`409`. The throttle's rate is
`REST_FRAMEWORK["DEFAULT_THROTTLE_RATES"]["popup_submissions"]`.

</Step>
