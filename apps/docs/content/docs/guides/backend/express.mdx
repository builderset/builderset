_This page describes Builderset 0.4.0._

At the end an Express server stores email templates, articles and forms, refuses an
unsafe or stale save with the editor's own issue codes, and takes form responses
checked by the same `validateAnswers` the form ran in the browser. There is no ORM:
the queries are written against the tables of the [SQL schema](/docs/guides/backend/sql),
and the routes are the ones in [Data model and API](/docs/guides/backend/data-model).
It needs `express`, `pg` and the three kits from npm. For the same routes as Next.js
route handlers with an ORM, see [Next.js and Drizzle](/docs/guides/backend/next-drizzle).

### One router for three builders

The routes are written once, in `documentRouter(kind)`. What differs between emails,
articles and forms is a small table: the table name, the kit's check, the columns
copied from the document's meta, what a new row needs. The table and column names in
the SQL come only from here, never from a request:

### Saving a draft

The editor sends back the `saved_at` it last saw. A different one on the row means
someone else saved in between: `409`, and the editor offers to reload or keep its
own. The update is conditional, so two saves at the same instant cannot both win:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="documents.ts"'>
  ./snippets/backends/express/documents.ts#draft
</include>

</Step>
<Step>

### Publishing

The strict check, then the document goes live with its meta copied to the columns,
in one transaction. Publishing is its own permission. An email type keeps one active
design; an article with a future `scheduled_at` waits for a job of yours to set it
live; a form's address taken by another is refused on `meta.slug`, where the editor
shows it:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="documents.ts"'>
  ./snippets/backends/express/documents.ts#publish
</include>

Creating, reading, the list and unpublishing are in the same router. Unpublishing
leaves an email or an article a draft, and a form `closed`: its link keeps working
and shows the closed message. Revisions and saved sections have their tables; their
routes are a list and an insert with the same check, and until you add them the
editor hides those features.

</Step>
<Step>

### The form's public routes

No sign-in. The answers are checked against the **published** document, never the
draft; a filled trap field is answered as a success and stored nowhere; and only the
`values` that `validateAnswers` keeps are stored, so an answer to a question the
visitor was not shown never reaches the table. The count goes up in the same
statement that checks `maxResponses`, so two visitors cannot both take the last
place:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="forms.ts"'>
  ./snippets/backends/express/forms.ts
</include>

</Step>
<Step>

### Uploads

An upload checks the file's type by its first bytes and its size, stores it under a
new key, and answers with the key and its address. The Next.js example's
[`lib/uploads.ts`](/docs/guides/backend/next-drizzle#uploads) is plain Node and works
here as it is, behind a multipart parser such as `multer`; a `media` row records
each file.

</Step>
