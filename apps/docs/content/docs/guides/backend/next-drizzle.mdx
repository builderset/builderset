---

At the end your Next.js app stores email templates in Postgres with who made, changed
and deleted each one, keeps their revisions and an activity log, refuses an unsafe or
stale save, and renders a published template to send. Every file below is from
[`examples/backends/next-drizzle`](https://github.com/builderset/builderset/tree/development/examples/backends/next-drizzle),
whose tests run every route against a real Postgres on each change. The same routes
are in [Prisma](/docs/guides/backend/next-prisma) and in
[FastAPI](/docs/guides/backend/fastapi-sqlalchemy); the tables are the email part of
the [data model](/docs/guides/backend/data-model).

_This page describes Builderset 0.4.0._

### Saving a draft

The editor sends back the `saved_at` it last saw. A different one on the row, or
none, means someone else may have saved in between: `409`, and the editor offers to
reload or keep its own (`force`). The row is locked first, so two saves at the same
moment take turns. A manual save also keeps a revision and an activity; an autosave
keeps neither:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="lib/emails.ts"'>
  ../../examples/backends/next-drizzle/lib/emails.ts#draft
</include>

The route only reads the request and calls it:

### Publishing

The strictly checked document goes live as the next `version`, with its `publish`
revision and its activity. One design per email type is active: the one before it
goes back to draft, and its timeline says what replaced it:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="lib/emails.ts"'>
  ../../examples/backends/next-drizzle/lib/emails.ts#publish
</include>

Unpublishing changes the status, not the document, so `saved_at` stays. Reading,
listing and creating are in the same file, and every route in
[`app/api/emails`](https://github.com/builderset/builderset/tree/development/examples/backends/next-drizzle/app/api/emails)
is a few lines like the draft's.

</Step>
<Step>

### The trash

everywhere but `restore`. A published template is what the mailer sends, so it is
unpublished first. `GET /api/emails?deleted=true` lists the trash:

`TEST_DATABASE_URL`. They never read `DATABASE_URL`, which may be one you care about.
