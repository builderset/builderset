At the end your writers can insert an element only your project has — a price, a
product card, a booking widget — and your site draws it. It is one definition,
shared by the editor, the renderer and the server.

_This page describes Builderset 0.5.0._

<Callout type="warn" title="Known limits in 0.5.0">
  The article editor has no `elements` prop yet, so the canvas draws nothing for a custom element
  while the writer edits it; its form in the inspector and your site show it fully. The email kit's
  `renderEmail` draws only its own blocks, so custom elements are for articles today. Both are
  planned.
</Callout>

## Define it

`defineElement` takes a type name, a version, a zod schema for its props and their
defaults. The schema is the contract: the editor builds the element's form from it,
and the checks refuse any document whose props do not match.

<include cwd lang="tsx" meta='title="price-tag.tsx"'>
  ./snippets/guides/custom-element.tsx#define
</include>

`z` is the zod the editor itself validates with; import it from `@builderset/engine`
so the two agree. When the props change shape, raise `version` and give the definition a `migrate`
that turns the old props into the new: stored documents are brought forward when
they are read ([Upgrading](/docs/guides/upgrading#stored-documents)).

## Draw it on your site

A component receives the element's props, already checked against the schema.
Pass it to the renderer by its type: `elements={{ 'price-tag': PriceTagView }}`.

## On your server

Nothing new: `parseArticle(body.document, registry)` and `checkArticle(document,
registry)` with the registry above validate your element's props like any other.
A document that carries an element your registry does not know is refused with
`element.unknown`.
