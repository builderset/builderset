At the end someone on your team builds a popup — a newsletter slide-in, a cookie
bar, an exit offer — and publishes it; your site shows it to the right visitors, at
the right moment, as often as it was told to; and your server checks every answer a
visitor sends with exactly the rules the popup used in the browser.

_This page describes Builderset 0.4.0, where the popup kit is new. Every element is in
[Popup elements](/docs/reference/popup-elements); the document, the rules and every
prop are in [Popup](/docs/reference/popup)._

## The editor

A client component, loaded only on the page that edits
([Frontend setup](/docs/guides/frontend)). `createRestHost` saves through your routes
([The host adapter](/docs/guides/host)):

<include cwd lang="tsx" meta='title="popup-edit.tsx"'>
  ./snippets/guides/popup-editor.tsx
</include>

- `record.status: 'published'` means the popup is **live**. The publish button calls
  your host's `publish`, and your server checks again.
- The popup is a free canvas in CSS pixels: each element sits at its own x and y —
  drag, resize, rotate, snap, align, layers. The settings hold its **format** (type,
  size, look), its **display** rules and its **motion**; the preview shows it on a
  made-up page, at a computer's or a phone's size, as a visitor meets it.
- `colors` puts your brand first in every colour field.

## Types

| Type         | What it is                                         | Dims the page | Takes the keyboard |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------ |
| `modal`      | A box in the middle, in three sizes                | yes           | yes                |
| `slidein`    | A small box in a corner                            | no            | no                 |
| `bartop`     | A bar across the top of the page                   | no            | no                 |
| `barbottom`  | A bar across the bottom                            | no            | no                 |
| `fullscreen` | The whole window                                   | yes           | yes                |
| `drawer`     | A panel down the left or the right side            | yes           | yes                |
| `inline`     | A card inside the page, where you put `PopupView`  | no            | no                 |

A bar or a full screen stretches to the page; its elements stay in a box of the
design's width, centred. A popup wider than the screen is scaled down to fit.

## Variables

A popup's words may hold variables — `{{visitor.first_name}}`, `{{offer.ends}}` —
that your page fills in. Which ones exist is yours to say, each with an example for
the editor's preview. Give the editor and your server the same list:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="popup-fields.ts"'>
  ./snippets/guides/popup-fields.ts
</include>

On the page, `data` holds the values, by path: `{ visitor: { first_name: 'Ana' } }`.
A value that is missing reads as nothing, so write words that still read well
without it.

## On your site

One component in your site's layout: it asks your server for the popups this page
may show, and draws each with `PopupView`. `popup.css` is plain CSS under `.bsp`,
with no reset — safe beside your site's own:

<include cwd lang="tsx" meta='title="site-popups.tsx"'>
  ./snippets/guides/popup-site.tsx#popups
</include>

What happens next is the popup's own settings:

- **Rules first.** It shows only on the devices, pages, dates, language and kind of
  visitor (new or returning) it was set for — and as often: every visit, once a
  visit, once every few days, or until the visitor closes it. Someone who filled it
  in is never asked again.
- **Then the trigger**: on load, after a delay, at a scroll depth, when the mouse
  heads for the tab bar, or on a click on an element of your page (a CSS selector,
  `#offer` — your own "Get the offer" button).
- **Then its motion** — fade, grow, slide up, from the side — and it closes with its
  ×, a "close" button, a click on the dimmed page, Esc, or by itself after a while.
  A modal takes the keyboard while it is open and gives it back after; under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves.
- **Its memory.** The browser remembers when it was shown, closed and filled in,
  under `storageKey` — your row's id — in `localStorage` by default. Pass `store`
  for somewhere else (`memoryStore()` in tests).
- **Its events.** `onEvent` is called with `'shown'`, `'closed'`, `'clicked'` and
  `'submitted'`, for your counts. `onClick` says which element was clicked (its id
  and its name in the layers, "Accept all"); `onClose` says why it closed.
- **Its inputs.** A button that sends checks the inputs first — required, an email
  address, a phone number, a plate — and calls `onSubmit` with the answers. Resolve
  `false` (or throw) and the visitor stays on the form with a message; anything else
  shows the thank-you.

For a cookie bar, use `onClick`: the element's layer name tells "Accept all" from
"Only necessary", and your page sets its consent from it.

- `checkPopup` and `check_popup` return the same issues with the same codes
  ([Issues](/docs/reference/issues)): no way to close it, a link that goes nowhere,
  inputs with no name or no button to send them, rules that can never match, a
  variable your pages do not fill in. Shown on every visit, and a modal the moment
  the page opens, are warnings — search engines rank such pages lower.
- `shouldShow` and `should_show` are the rules the popup applies in the browser, as
  one pure function: the server and the page give the same answer
  ([Popup](/docs/reference/popup#display-rules)). `pickPopup` chooses one of
  several by priority.
- `validateSubmission` and `validate_submission` return the answers worth keeping
  — the popup's own inputs only, trimmed — or, per input, the first thing wrong.
- The tables — `popup`, its revisions, `popup_submission` and `popup_daily_stat`,
  which counts each day's events — and the public routes the site above calls are in
  [Data model and API](/docs/guides/backend/data-model#the-routes).

## Templates

The editor's Templates tab offers fourteen popups for seven purposes — lead capture,
offers and sales, announcements, newsletter, cookies and consent, feedback, app
download — across the seven types. For a "New popup" screen of your own,
`POPUP_TEMPLATES` lists them and `buildTemplatePopup(registry, template)` builds one
as a whole document, both from `@builderset/popup/editor`. Their words are in
Albanian, and are reworded and translated in the editor.

## Languages

The editor speaks English, Albanian and German (`locales={{ sq, de }}`). A popup can
carry its words in several languages with `contentLocales`; pass the same list to
`checkPopup(…, { locales })`. On the site, `locale` picks the popup's words and the
few the popup does not set itself — the close button's name, the countdown's units,
what is wrong with an answer — built in for `en`, `sq` and `de`; pass `words` for
another language. A display rule can limit a popup to one language: the page's
`<html lang>` decides.
