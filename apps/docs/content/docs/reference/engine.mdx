_This page describes Builderset 0.4.0._

A project uses a kit and rarely needs this page. A [custom element](/docs/guides/custom-elements)
is an `ElementDefinition`; a new kit (pages, slides) is a set of them plus an
`EditorProfile`.

## An element

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="@builderset/engine — ElementDefinition"'>
  ../../packages/engine/src/core/registry.ts#element-definition

`defineElement(definition)` types one; `new ElementRegistry(definitions)` holds a
set, and `registry.get(type)` and `registry.all()` read it.

## The operations

Every change to a document is an operation on its tree, from
`@builderset/engine`: `insert`, `move`, `remove`, `duplicate`, `update`,
`wrap`, `unwrap` and `replaceType`. Each checks the registry's placement rules. Run through `applyChange` or `History`,
a change records the patches that undo it. A script that changes
documents should use them rather than edit the JSON by hand.

## A kit's editor

The workspace — panels, undo, drag and drop, the inspector — is the same for every
kind of document. A kit supplies the parts that are not:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="@builderset/engine/editor — EditorProfile"'>
  ../../packages/engine/src/editor/profile.ts#profile
</include>

Most of it is one function or component. Five parts change what kind of editor it is:

| Part             | What it does                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `canvasFrame`    | `page` (an article's reading measure), `bare` (the renderer draws the whole page: an email's backdrop), `none` (the kit draws its own card: a form) |
| `stage`          | The whole board in place of the page canvas: a [free canvas](#a-free-canvas), elements at their own x and y — a card, a popup              |
| `layersPanel`    | The left rail's Layers tab, in place of the tree: a free canvas lists the topmost element first                                          |
| `nodePanel`      | The inspector's body for one selected element, in place of the shared Content, Style and Advanced tabs. Return `null` to keep them       |
| `selectionPanel` | Drawn in the inspector above the actions on several selected elements: aligning and spreading them                                       |

## A free canvas

For a kit whose elements are not a flow of blocks but sit at their own place — a
card's logo, a popup's button. The engine draws the artboard, the elements (through
the kit's `renderElement`), the selection with its eight handles and the rotate
handle, the snap guides, the marquee, the drop from the library, the layers, and the
arrange and layout fields. The kit supplies the document, the elements' look and its
units. The [card](/docs/guides/card) and [popup](/docs/guides/popup) kits are built
this way.

### The document

The artboard is a container node — a card's side, a popup's root. Its children are
the placed elements, in stacking order: the last one is on top. Every placed element
has `props.frame`, in the kit's units from the artboard's top-left corner, turned
clockwise about its centre:

<include cwd lang="ts" meta='title="@builderset/engine — FreeFrame"'>
  ../../packages/engine/src/core/frame.ts#frame
</include>

Put `frame: freeFrameSchema` in the element's props schema and
`frame: { ...UNPLACED_FRAME }` in its defaults: a frame with no size has not been
placed yet, and `placeNewNodes` gives it one when it is added. The node's own
`hidden`, `locked` and `name` are used: hidden elements are dimmed on the stage,
locked ones can be selected but not moved, and the name is the layer's.

<include cwd lang="tsx" meta='title="sticker.tsx"'>

</include>

`frameOf`, `frameBounds`, `isPlaced` and `normalizeAngle` are in the core too, for a
kit's checks and its server-side renderer: no React.

### The stage and its panels

`FreeStage` takes the artboard (its id and size, corners, background, bleed, extra
snap lines, and layers under and over the elements that never catch the pointer),
the unit, and how to draw an element. Inside the artboard one unit is `unit.px` CSS
pixels at every zoom: the kit draws at natural size. `FreeLayersPanel` is the Layers
tab, `FreeArrange` aligns and spreads a selection, and `FreeLayoutFields` edits one
element's place, size, rotation, lock and name:

### What the stage does

- **Select**: click; Shift, Ctrl or ⌘ adds or removes; drag on the empty artboard
  for a marquee; Escape clears; Ctrl/⌘ A selects every visible, unlocked element.
- **Move**: drag. Shift keeps to one axis; Alt does not snap. The selection snaps to
  the artboard's edges and centre, to `snapX`/`snapY` and to the other elements,
  with guides, and a tooltip says where it is in the unit ("X 12.5 mm Y 4 mm").
- **Resize** with eight handles, also on a rotated element; Shift or `keepRatio`
  keeps the proportions. **Rotate** with the round handle: it snaps to 0, 45, 90…;
  Shift snaps to 15° steps.
- **Keyboard**: the arrows nudge by `unit.nudge`, Shift by `unit.bigNudge`, and a
  held arrow is one undo step; Enter or a double-click edits text (`editable`);
  Ctrl/⌘ ] and [ restack. Each element is focusable and the stage is a labelled
  region; moves are announced.
- **Drop** from the library: a box the element's size (`sizeOf`) follows the
  pointer, snapping as a move does, and the element lands at that frame.
- One drag, resize, rotate or marquee is **one undo step**.

The functions under the panels work on a draft, one undo step each inside
`updateDocument`: `placeNewNodes`, `alignNodes`, `distributeNodes`, `moveFramesBy`
and `writeFrame`; `useFreeCommands(decimals)` wraps them for a component. The
geometry — `snapAxis`, `resizeFrame`, `rotateFrom`, `unionBounds`, `formatUnit` — is
pure. The words are `messages.free.*` in the editor's locales; a host overrides any
through `messages`.
